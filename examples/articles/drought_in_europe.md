*Reporting date: 19 October 2026. Measurements and assessments refer to the dates stated below.*

On 5 October, the Danube at Devín, near Bratislava, recorded a daily average flow of just **575.9 cubic metres per second**. Slovakia's hydrometeorological institute, SHMÚ, identified it as the lowest since measurements began in November 1900, below the previous minimum of 580 recorded in January 1909. These are preliminary operational measurements for one station, but they show how exceptional this autumn's low flows have become. [SHMÚ's assessment](https://www.shmu.sk/sk/index.php?id=1787&page=2049).

### A summer deficit that continued into autumn

The Danube record followed a continent-wide low-flow summer. Copernicus reported the lowest summer flows in its EFAS record, which begins in 1992. Its comparison combines mean June–August discharge at the outlets of 12 major rivers. That places 2026 below earlier drought summers within this record, including 2018 and 2022; it does not establish a minimum for every European river throughout history. [Copernicus summer assessment](https://climate.copernicus.eu/copernicus-record-heat-stress-and-low-river-flows-marked-europes-2026-summer).

Record or near-record flows occurred across the Rhine, Danube, Po, Loire, Rhône and Seine in July and August. By mid-October, national reports still described substantial deficits, although the timing and extent of recovery varied between catchments.

### Why so little water is reaching the rivers

A river depends on rainfall across its catchment, groundwater entering the channel and, in mountain-fed systems, melting snow and ice. A prolonged rainfall shortage reduces these inputs. High temperatures increase evaporative demand, leaving less water available to replenish soils, groundwater and rivers.

Switzerland's Federal Office for the Environment links the current drought to rainfall shortages since spring and a winter with little snow. It also explains why occasional showers have provided limited relief: streams can respond quickly, while deeper soils, aquifers and springs recover much more slowly. [Swiss assessment, 16 October](https://www.bafu.admin.ch/de/sommer-2026-trockenheit-und-hohe-wassertemperaturen).

Climate change adds pressure through warming and changes in snow storage. The European Environment Agency expects reduced snow cover and earlier melting to worsen water-related extremes, with particularly severe water stress in southern Europe. This regional outlook does not attribute every individual low-flow record solely to climate change. [EEA assessment](https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/europe-environment-2025/thematic-briefings/biodiversity-and-ecosystems/water-and-climate-impacts).

### What the national reports show

The following snapshots describe different places and indicators. Gauge height, river discharge and reservoir storage measure different things; their values cannot be ranked as a single drought score.

| Country | Dated evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Germany | At Maxau on the Upper Rhine, daily mean discharge was **369 m³/s on 13 October**; gauge height was 167 cm below the 1991–2020 average for that date. On the Elbe, sustained low water triggered additional monitoring on 6 August. [Baden-Württemberg report](https://niz.baden-wuerttemberg.de/lageberichte/oberflaechengewaesser), [BfG Elbe monitoring](https://undine.bafg.de/elbe/extremereignisse/elbe_mp_extremereignisse.html). |
| France | The **7 October** Rhône-Mediterranean and Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes bulletin described continuing flow declines and dry or weakly flowing streams in several areas. Groundwater in the Loire and Allier alluvial aquifers remained low to very low. Elsewhere in the same reporting area, intense Mediterranean rainfall was causing floods. [Regional hydrological bulletin](https://www.rhone-mediterranee.eaufrance.fr/sites/sierm/files/content/2026-10/20261007_bulletin_secheresse_bassin_rhmed_region_aura_vpublic.pdf). |
| Poland | IMGW's **15 October** assessment counted **305 stations below the mean low-flow threshold**, down from 339 a week earlier. Its forecast still placed much of the Vistula and sections of the Oder in low-water conditions. [IMGW weekly report](https://imgw.pl/en/prognoza-pogody-na-tydzien-16-10-22-10-2026/). |
| United Kingdom | England's monitored reservoir storage was **54% of capacity in the week ending 13 October**, with 18 reservoir groups below half full. This is an England figure, rather than a UK-wide total. [Environment Agency report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dry-weather-and-drought-in-england-2026-summary-reports/dry-weather-and-drought-in-england-9-to-15-october-2026). |
| Italy | The Po basin authority's **16 October** update classified the preceding 30 days as severe streamflow drought at Piacenza and Pontelagoscuro. At Pontelagoscuro, discharge on 15 October was **659 m³/s**, above the authority's 450 m³/s saltwater-intrusion threshold. [Po basin update](https://www.adbpo.it/aggiornamento-settimanale-situazione-idrologica-16-10-2026/). |
| Spain | MITECO's **14 October** release put reservoir storage at **57.8% of capacity**. Basin figures ranged from 45.4% in the Ebro to 74.9% in Guadalete-Barbate. These percentages show regional differences in stored water; they do not alone establish the severity of river drought. [MITECO reservoir report](https://www.miteco.gob.es/es/prensa/ultimas-noticias/2026/octubre/la-reserva-hidrica-espanola-se-encuentra-al-57-8---de-su-capacid.html). |

Reservoir levels also reflect releases, water use and operating rules. The Environment Agency notes that maintenance and flood-storage requirements affect some reservoirs, so a low storage figure needs context before it is interpreted as a rainfall deficit.

### Low flows also change river habitat

For aquatic life, water temperature matters alongside flow. Smaller watercourses can warm rapidly during hot, dry conditions, and warmer water holds less oxygen. Species dependent on cool, oxygen-rich water are particularly vulnerable. [Swiss environmental assessment](https://www.bafu.admin.ch/de/sommer-2026-trockenheit-und-hohe-wassertemperaturen).

France's 7 October bulletin reported substantial fish and amphibian mortality in affected parts of Grand Est and Bourgogne-Franche-Comté. [Regional ecological observations](https://www.rhone-mediterranee.eaufrance.fr/sites/sierm/files/content/2026-10/20261007_bulletin_secheresse_bassin_rhmed_region_aura_vpublic.pdf).

Local measurements remain essential: Baden-Württemberg's 14 October report described extreme low water while reporting oxygen levels in the Neckar, Rhine and Danube as uncritical. Low flow increases ecological pressure without producing identical water-quality conditions everywhere.

### Will winter restore the missing water?

Autumn and winter rainfall are important for groundwater recharge, but recovery depends on sustained precipitation reaching depleted stores. A brief rise in river level cannot establish that deeper reserves have recovered.

The UK's September hydrological summary illustrates the uncertainty. Rainfall was near average nationally, yet Scotland and Northern Ireland were wetter than average while much of England stayed substantially drier. Although seasonal forecasts favoured wetter conditions over the following three months, the outlook still indicated below-normal river flows and groundwater levels in parts of south-east England. [National River Flow Archive summary](https://nrfa.ceh.ac.uk/news-and-media/news/hydrological-summary-september-2026-published).

That regional forecast cannot be extended to the whole continent. Seasonal models describe possible departures from average conditions; they do not guarantee rainfall for an individual catchment. [ECMWF seasonal forecast guidance](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/documentation-and-support/seasonal).

Europe enters the recharge season with substantial deficits in many river basins. Over the coming months, the important evidence will be whether rainfall produces sustained improvements in flows, groundwater and storage. Longer-term assessments point to increasing drought pressure, especially in southern Europe, while the pace of recovery from the present episode remains dependent on where and when rain falls. [EEA outlook](https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/europe-environment-2025/thematic-briefings/biodiversity-and-ecosystems/water-and-climate-impacts).
